Section V in one page: a position-only clock

This is the intentionally simplified replacement for the long derivation. The full version was preserved as transient_law_derivation_full_preserved.html. Assumption for this page: $c=1$.

The cartoon claim. If the walk is currently a distance $d$ from the maximum, the transient-correlation half-time is approximately

$$\boxed{\tau(d)=\tau_{\max}\left(1-e^{-d/d_*}\right)}$$

For the two Fig. D5 examples, a single normalized fit is already good enough:

$$\boxed{\tau(d)\approx 0.500\,N\left(1-e^{-d/(0.587\,N)}\right)}.$$

1. What survives from the full law

The exact-looking law had three distances and a complicated pool-memory factor $\rho_p$. For prediction, Section V plugs in the typical future path: $$u_{12}(\Delta t)\approx \Delta t,\qquad d_2(\Delta t)\approx d-v_{\rm rem}\Delta t.$$ When you differentiate the resulting curve at $\Delta t=0$, the two terms involving $v_{\rm rem}$ cancel. That is the useful part. It says:

$$\text{clock at depth }d \;\propto\; 1-\rho_p(d).$$

In words: the remaining transient variance sets the time scale. Near the maximum there is little variance left, so the clock is short. Far away, the pool has already forgotten most of the maximum, so the clock cannot grow forever.

2. How to simplify $\rho_p$ consistently

The detailed $\rho_p(d)$ knows about Krawtchouk factors, flipped-spin signs, clipping, and adaptivity. To explain Fig. D5 rather than re-derive it, keep only the shape it must have:

  • $\rho_p(0)=1$: at zero distance, the spectrum is perfectly correlated with itself.
  • $\rho_p(d)$ decreases as $d$ grows.
  • It has one effective memory length.

The simplest function with those properties is a single exponential, $$\rho_p(d)\approx e^{-d/d_*}.$$ Substituting this into "clock $\propto 1-\rho_p(d)$" gives the saturation law above.

What got swept under the rug? The difference between a tangent time and a half-time, the exact small-distance slope, the $p$-dependence, and the clipping of the original $\rho_p$. All of that is now absorbed into the two fitted constants $\tau_{\max}$ and $d_*$. This is less exact and much easier to remember.

3. Can $d_*$ be derived?

Yes, but only after deciding what the simplified exponential is trying to match.

If the exponential is replacing $\rho_p$ itself, the small-distance slope gives a clean derivation. The detailed law has $$\rho_p'(0)=-\Lambda/N,\qquad \Lambda=2(p-1)+4.$$ Matching $e^{-d/d_*}$ to that slope gives $$d_*^{(\rho)}=\frac{N}{\Lambda}.$$ This is the microscopic memory length of the pool law. It belongs to the tangent-time formula $$\tau_{\rm tan}(d)=\frac{2N}{\Lambda}\left(1-e^{-d/(N/\Lambda)}\right).$$

If the exponential is replacing the Fig. D5 half-time curve, two extra effects enter: the half-time is shorter than the tangent time, and the far-field half-time saturates near $N/4$ because the clipped flip factor in $\rho_p(u)$ hits zero at $u=N/4$. In the deep basin, the counting formula gives $$\rho(\Delta t)\approx \frac{2d-(1+v_{\rm rem})\Delta t}{2\sqrt{d(d-v_{\rm rem}\Delta t)}}.$$ Setting this to $1/2$ gives $t_{1/2}\approx \alpha(v_{\rm rem})d$, where $$\alpha(v)= \frac{(4+3v)-\sqrt{(4+3v)^2-12(1+v)^2}}{2(1+v)^2}.$$ Matching that slope to a saturation curve with plateau $N/4$ gives $$d_*^{(1/2)}\approx \frac{N}{4\alpha(v_{\rm rem})}\approx 0.33N.$$ That is an analytic, endpoint-matched estimate for the half-time version.

The fitted $d_*=0.587N$ above is therefore not a fundamental length. It is an effective midrange parameter: it is what you get when both $\tau_{\max}$ and $d_*$ are allowed to float so that one red curve shadows the full d-only law over the plotted depths. For derivation, $N/\Lambda$ is the clean answer for $\rho_p$; roughly $N/3$ is the endpoint-matched answer for the half-time cartoon.

5. The shortest usable recipe

  1. Count the currently beneficial moves, $n_+$.
  2. Use the right panel calibration to translate $n_+$ into an estimated depth $\hat d$.
  3. Forecast the transient half-time with $$\tau(\hat d)\approx 0.5N\left(1-e^{-\hat d/(0.59N)}\right).$$

That is the whole simplified Section V: depth is the clock, the clock grows roughly linearly near the maximum, and it saturates at about half the system size in accepted flips.
